## In a fully allocation problem with infinitely divisible social endowment $\displaystyle M \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}$ and strictly monotonic preferences, pairwise efficiency implies efficiency. If for all $\displaystyle i,j \in N$, the pair $\displaystyle (x_{i},x_{j})$ is an efficient way of distributing $\displaystyle x_{i}+x_{j}$ among them, then $\displaystyle x$ is an efficient allocation.

### If the social endowment $\displaystyle M \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{1}$ is a single-dimensional good, then any full allocation $\displaystyle x$ is Pareto efficient and the pairwise efficiency condition is redundant.

By the way of contradiction, suppose $\displaystyle x$ is a pair-efficient allocation but is not efficient, i,e. there is another allocation $\displaystyle y$ Pareto dominating $\displaystyle x$:
$$
\begin{align}
\forall i \in N & &  u_{i}\left( y_{i} \right)\geq u_{i}\left( x_{i} \right)  &  & \implies y_{i} \geq x_{i}\\
\exists i^{\star} \in N &  & u_{i^{\star}}\left( y_{i^{\star}} \right) > u_{i^{\star}}\left( x_{i^{\star}} \right) &  & \implies y_{i^{\star}} > x_{i^{\star}}\\
\end{align}
$$
where the right hand side is induced from monotonic preference on single-dimensional good. 
Take the summation on the inequalities, we have
$$
\sum_{i \in N}y_{i}>\sum_{i \in N}x_{i}=M
$$
which is a contradiction with the feasible allocation.

### If the social endowment $\displaystyle M \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{L>1}$ is a multiple-dimensional good, the pairwise efficiency does imply efficiency.

By the way of contradiction, suppose $\displaystyle x$ is a pair-efficient allocation but is not efficient, i,e. there is another allocation $\displaystyle y$ Pareto dominating $\displaystyle x$:
$$
\begin{align}
\forall i \in N & &  u_{i}\left( y_{i} \right)\geq u_{i}\left( x_{i} \right)  \\
\exists i^{\star} \in N &  & u_{i^{\star}}\left( y_{i^{\star}} \right) > u_{i^{\star}}\left( x_{i^{\star}} \right) \\
\end{align}
$$
Take any pair $\displaystyle (i,j) \in N^{2}$, claim:
1. If $\displaystyle u_{i}\left( y_{i} \right)=u_{i}\left( x_{i} \right)  \cap  u_{j}\left( y_{j} \right)=u_{j}\left( x_{j} \right)$, then $\displaystyle y_{i}+y_{j}\geq x_{i}+x_{j}$
	Otherwise, suppose $\displaystyle y_{i}+y_{j}<x_{i}+x_{j}$, we can easily construct an alternative allocation $\displaystyle z =\left( z_{i}=y_{i}, z_{j}=y_{j}+\left( x_{i}+x_{j}-y_{i}-y_{j} \right)>y_{j} \right)$. By strict monotone of preference, $\displaystyle z$ Pareto dominates $\displaystyle x$ within the pair $\displaystyle (i,j)$, contradicting the pairwise efficiency.
2. If $\displaystyle u_{i}\left( y_{i} \right)>u_{i}\left( x_{i} \right)  \cap  u_{j}\left( y_{j} \right)=u_{j}\left( x_{j} \right)$, then $\displaystyle y_{i}+y_{j}> x_{i}+x_{j}$
	Otherwise, suppose $\displaystyle y_{i}+y_{j}\leq x_{i}+x_{j}$, we can easily construct an alternative allocation $\displaystyle z =\left( z_{i}=y_{i}+\left( x_{i}+x_{j}-y_{i}-y_{j} \right)\geq y_{i}, z_{j}=y_{j} \right)$. By monotone of preference, $\displaystyle z$ Pareto dominates $\displaystyle x$ within the pair $\displaystyle (i,j)$, contradicting the pairwise efficiency.

Take summation on all pairs $\displaystyle (i,j)$, we have
$$
\sum_{\left( i,j \right)}\left( y_{i}+y_{j} \right)=\left( N-1 \right)\sum_{i \in N}y_{i}>\sum_{\left( i,j \right)}\left( x_{i}+x_{j} \right)=\left( N-1 \right)\sum_{i \in N}x_{i}=\left( N-1 \right)M
$$
which is a contradiction with the feasible allocation.

## In an object allocation problem, leave-one-out efficiency does not imply efficiency. If for all $\displaystyle i \in N$, the allocation $\displaystyle x_{-i}$ is efficient for the problem with agent $\displaystyle N-\left\{ i \right\}$ and social endowment $\displaystyle \cap_{j \neq i}x_{j}$, then $\displaystyle x$ is not necessarily an efficient allocation.

The following is a counterexample. The key is by dropping any agent with his endowment, the origin trading cycle is broken, inducing the inefficiency. 

| Preference                    | Agent 1 | Agent 2 | Agent 3 | ... | Agent N-1 | Agent N |
| ----------------------------- | ------- | ------- | ------- | --- | --------- | ------- |
| 1st priority                  | 2       | 3       | 4       |     | N         | 1       |
| 2nd priority                  | 1       | 2       | 3       |     | N-1       | N       |
| Pairwise efficient allocation | 1       | 2       | 3       |     | N-1       | N       |
| Efficient allocation          | 2       | 3       | 4       |     | N         | 1       |
