## Manipulation game: 
Set of agent $\displaystyle i \in \{ 1, 2 \}$ with true preference $\displaystyle u_{i}=x_{1}^{a_{i}}x_{2}^{1-a_{1}}$.
Set of strategies $\displaystyle \beta_{i} \in \mathcal{R}$ is the collation of all possible preferences.
Payoff function $\displaystyle P\left( \beta \right)=W\left( \beta=\left( \beta_{i} \right)_{i \in \left\{ 1,2 \right\}}, E=\left( E^{i}\in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2} \right)_{i \in\left\{ 1,2 \right\}}  \right)$ is the Walrasian rule with preference $\displaystyle \beta$ and endowment $\displaystyle E$.
For each agent $\displaystyle i$, he choose a strategy $\displaystyle \beta_{i}$ (a fake preference) to maximize his utility $\displaystyle u_{i}\left( P^{i}\left( \beta \right) \right)$ after the realization of Walrasian rule.

## Nash Equilibrium with restriction $\displaystyle \mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{CD}$

### A simplified case: $\displaystyle E=\left( E^{i} \right)$ where $\displaystyle E^{1}=\left( e_{1},0 \right)$ and $\displaystyle E^{2}=\left( 0,e_{2} \right)$
The offer curve is a vertical or horizontal line.
The Nash equilibrium is $\displaystyle \beta_{1}=\left( u_{1}^{L}=x_{1} \right)$ and $\displaystyle \beta_{2}=\left( u^{L}_{2}=x_{2} \right)$ and the result is $\displaystyle W\left( \beta,E \right)=E$, i.e. both agents consume their endowment and their utilities are 0.

### A general case: $\displaystyle E=\left( E^{i} \right)$ where $\displaystyle E^{1}=\left( e_{1},e_{2} \right)>0$ and $\displaystyle E^{2}=\left( w_{1}-e_{1}, w_{2}-e_{2} \right)$
Given announced preferences $\displaystyle \beta=\left( \beta_{1} =x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}},\beta_{2}=y_{1}^{b_{1}}y_{2}^{b_{2}}\right)$ , with normalization $\displaystyle a_{1}+a_{2}=1, b_{1}+b_{2}=1$ and endowment $\displaystyle E=\left( E^{1}=\left( e_{1},e_{2} \right),E^{2}=\left( w_{1}-e_{1},w_{2}-e_{2} \right) \right)$, the Walrasian rule suggests:
$$
\begin{align}
x_{1}=a_{1}e_{1} \left( 1+\frac{p_{2}e_{2}}{p_{1}e_{1}} \right)  & &  y_{1}=b_{1}\left( w_{1}-e_{1} \right)\left( 1+\frac{p_{2}\left( w_{2}-e_{2} \right)}{p_{1}\left( w_{1}-e_{1} \right)} \right) \\
x_{2}=a_{2}e_{2}\left( \frac{p_{1}e_{1}}{p_{2}e_{2}} +1\right) &  & y_{2}=b_{2}\left( w_{2}-e_{2} \right)\left( \frac{p_{1}\left( w_{1}-e_{1} \right)}{p_{2}\left( w_{2}-e_{2} \right)}+1 \right)
\end{align}
$$
where $\displaystyle \frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}$ is defined by the following:
$$
\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}} = \frac{b_{2}w_{1}+\left( b_{1}-a_{1} \right)e_{1}}{b_{1}w_{2}-\left( b_{1}-a_{1} \right)e_{2}}
$$
Given agent $\displaystyle b$ 's announcement $\displaystyle \beta_{2}=y_{1}^{b_{1}}y_{2}^{b_{2}}$, agent $\displaystyle a$ chooses $\displaystyle \beta_{1}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{y_{2}}$ to maximize his true utility $\displaystyle u_{a}=x_{1}^{at_{1}}x_{2}^{at_{2}}$:
$$
\begin{align}
\left( a_{1}^{\star},a_{2}^{\star} \right) =   & \mathop{\arg\max}\limits_{\left( a_{1},a_{2} \right)}    x_{1}^{at_{1}}x_{2}^{at_{2}} \\
s.t.  &  x_{1}=a_{1}e_{1} \left( 1+\frac{p_{2}e_{2}}{p_{1}e_{1}} \right) \\
 & x_{2}=a_{2}e_{2}\left( \frac{p_{1}e_{1}}{p_{2}e_{2}} +1\right) \\
 & \frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}} = \frac{b_{2}w_{1}+\left( b_{1}-a_{1} \right)e_{1}}{b_{1}w_{2}-\left( b_{1}-a_{1} \right)e_{2}}
\end{align}
$$
which is equivalent to the following
$$
\begin{align}
\left( a_{1}^{\star},a_{2}^{\star} \right) =   & \mathop{\arg\max}\limits_{\left( a_{1},a_{2} \right)}    \left[a_{1}e_{1} \left( 1+\frac{p_{2}e_{2}}{p_{1}e_{1}} \right)\right]^{at_{1}}\left[a_{2}e_{2}\left( \frac{p_{1}e_{1}}{p_{2}e_{2}} +1\right)\right]^{at_{2}} \\
s.t.  & \frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}} = \frac{b_{2}w_{1}+\left( b_{1}-a_{1} \right)e_{1}}{b_{1}w_{2}-\left( b_{1}-a_{1} \right)e_{2}} \\
\end{align}
$$
Symmetrically, agent $\displaystyle b$ 's announcement $\displaystyle (b_{1}^{\star},b_{2}^{\star})$ should also be the best response to agent a 's announcement $\displaystyle (a_{1},a_{2})$. Finally, we could pin down the Nash equilibrium: 
$$
\begin{align}
\left( a_{1}^{\star}, a_{2}^{\star} \right)=BR\left( b_{1}^{\star},b_{2}^{\star} \right)  &  & \left( b_{1}^{\star},b_{2}^{\star} \right) = BR\left( a_{1}^{\star}, a_{2}^{\star} \right)
\end{align}
$$

![[Pasted image 20250421175356.png]]
With endowment $\displaystyle\left( e_{1},e_{2} \right)$ and Cobb-Douglas parameters $\displaystyle (a_{1},a_{2})$, agent $\displaystyle a$ 's offer curve is a hyperbola in the form: $\displaystyle \left(\frac{x_{1}}{a_{1}e_{1}}-1\right) \left(\frac{x_{2}}{a_{2}e_{2}}-1\right)=1$ crossing the endowment point $\displaystyle E$. Symmetrically, agent $\displaystyle b$'s offer curve is another hyperbola crossing the endowment point $\displaystyle E$. Usually, the 2 offer curves have 2 intersections and the Walrasian rule is the intersection different with $\displaystyle E$. 
Given agent $\displaystyle b$'s offer curve, agent $\displaystyle a$ is trying to announce a "fake" offer curve which could intersect with $\displaystyle b$'s offer curve on the top-right region. Symmetrically, agent $\displaystyle b$ is also trying to make his offer curve intersecting with $\displaystyle a$'s offer curve on the bottom-left region. Finally, the Nash equilibrium is $\displaystyle \beta=\left( \beta_{1}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}},\beta_{2}=y_{1}^{b_{1}}y_{2}^{b_{2}} \right)$ with $\displaystyle \frac{a_{1}e_{2}}{a_{2}e_{1}}=\frac{b_{1}\left( w_{2}-e_{2} \right)}{b_{2}\left( w_{1}-e_{1} \right)}$ such that the 2 offer curves contingent at their endowment point $\displaystyle E$. And the equilibrium allocation is the endowment allocation itself.

## All the Nash equilibrium allocation is in the intersection of 2 agents' upper contour sets: $\displaystyle NE \subset U^{1}\left( E^{1} \right) \cap U^{2}\left( E^{2} \right)$ with classical preferences.
From the first welfare theorem, Walrasian rule leads to a Pareto efficient allocation and $\displaystyle NE \subset X-\left( L^{1}\left( E^{1} \right) \cap L^{2}\left( E^{2} \right) \right)$, where $\displaystyle X$ represents the collection of all feasible allocations. The following is to show $\displaystyle NE \not\subset L^{i}\left( E^{i} \right) \cap U^{j}\left( E^{j} \right)$.
By the way of contradiction, suppose there is a Nash equilibrium allocation $\displaystyle x \in L^{1}\left( E^{1} \right) \cap U^{2}\left( E^{2} \right)$, where agents have strategy $\displaystyle \beta=\left( \beta_{1}, \beta_{2} \right)$. From definition of Walrasian rule, the segment connecting $\displaystyle x$ and $\displaystyle E$ separates 2 upper contour set defined by announced preferences $\displaystyle \beta$. Based on classical assumption on strict monotone and strict convexity, the segment $\displaystyle (x,E^{1})$ is in the strict contour set of $\displaystyle x$ for agent 1: $\displaystyle (x,E^{1}) \in SU^{1}\left( x \right)$. 
![[Pasted image 20250421175436.png]]
If agent 1 announces his true preference $\displaystyle u_{1}$, the new allocation $\displaystyle x'$ must be in the strict upper contour set of $\displaystyle x$: $\displaystyle x' \in SU^{1}\left(  x\right)$. Thus, at current Nash equilibrium $\displaystyle \beta$, agent 1 has incentive to deviate, leading to a contradiction.
