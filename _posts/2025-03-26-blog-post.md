# There is a pair $\left( u_{1}, u_{2} \right): \mathbb{R}_{+}\to \mathbb{R}_{+}$ and $M\in \mathbb{R}_{+}$ such that the image of the feasible set of the economy $\left( u_{1}, u_{2}, M \right)$ is $S$. 


For any convex and compact subset $S \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$, define the smallest convex and comprehensive hull containing $S$ as $con.comp\left( S \right):=\left\{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{2} \text{ : } x_{i}\leq D_{i}\left( S \right) \text{, } \forall i = 1, 2 \right\}$, where the maximal utility of each agent $i$ is defined as $D_{i}\left( S \right):=max\left\{ x_{i} \text{ : } x \in S \right\}$. Since there is at least one point x such that $x>0$, we have $D_{i}\left( S \right)>0$ and effective feasible set $con.comp\left( S \right) \cap \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$ is non-degenerate (a 2-dimensional set).

Denote the function form of the frontier of the effective feasible set $con.comp\left( S \right) \cap \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$ as $\Phi\left( v_{1}, v_{2} \right)=0$, where $\left( v_{1}, v_{2} \right) \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$ represents the assigned utilities to agent 1 and 2. 

For 1-good bargaining problem, each allocation on resource $\left( x_{1}, x_{2} \right)$ such that $x_{1}+x_{2}=M$ is Pareto Optimal and the assigned utility $\left( u_{1}\left( x_{1} \right),u_{2}\left( x_{2} \right) \right)$ is on the frontier of the feasible set. Thus, we have

$$
\begin{align*}
& v_{1} = u_{1}\left( M-u_{2}^{-1}\left( v_{2} \right) \right) \implies \Phi\left( u_{1}\left( M-u_{2}^{-1}\left( v_{2} \right) \right), v_{2} \right)=0 \\
& v_{2} = u_{2}\left( M-u_{1}^{-1}\left( v_{1} \right) \right) \implies \Phi\left( v_{1},u_{2}\left( M-u_{1}^{-1}\left( v_{1} \right) \right) \right)=0
\end{align*}
$$

Denote $u_{1}\left( x_{1} \right)=v_{1}$, $u_{2}\left( x_{2} \right)=v_{2}$ and $x_{1}+x_{2}=M$. Then differentiate the above 2 equations, we have

$$
\Phi_{1}\left( v_{1},v_{2} \right) \frac{u_{1}'\left( x_{1} \right)}{u_{2}'\left( x_{2} \right)} = \Phi_{2}\left( v_{1},v_{2} \right)
$$

Applying $x_{1}+x_{2}=M$, we finally get a system on 1st order ODEs:

$$
\begin{align*}
&\Phi_{1}\left( u_{1},u_{2} \right) u_{1}'\left( x_{1} \right)  - \Phi_{2}\left( u_{1},u_{2} \right) u_{2}'\left( M-x_{1} \right) =0 \\
&\Phi_{1}\left( u_{1},u_{2} \right) u_{1}'\left( M- x_{2} \right)  - \Phi_{2}\left( u_{1},u_{2} \right) u_{2}'\left( x_{2} \right) =0 
\end{align*}
$$

Given initial condition $u_{i}\left( 0 \right)=0$, $\forall i =1,2$, both $u_{1}$ and $u_{2}$ are solvable from the above system:

$$
\begin{align}
v_{1}=u_{1}\left( x_{1};M \right) && v_{2}=u_{2}\left( x_{2};M \right)
\end{align}
$$

Evaluate utilities at the corner point:

$$
\Phi\left( u_{1}\left( x_{1}=M;M \right),u_{2}\left( x_{2}=0;M \right) \right)=0
$$

We could finally pin down $M$.

Therefore, there is a pair $\left( u_{1},u_{2},M \right)$ such that the image of this economy is the feasible set $con.comp\left( S \right)$ with the origin as its disagreement point. 


# There is not always a pair $\left( u_{1}, u_{2} \right): \mathbb{R}_{+}\to \mathbb{R}_{+}$ and $M, M'\in \mathbb{R}_{+}$ such that the image of the feasible set of the economy $\left( u_{1}, u_{2}, M \right)$ is $S$ and the image of the feasible set of $\left( u_{1}, u_{2}, M' \right)$ is $S'$.


Here is a counterexample.

```latex
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=2]
% Axes
\draw[->] (0,0) -- (2.2,0) node[right] {$u_1$};
\draw[->] (0,0) -- (0,1.2) node[above] {$u_2$};

% S region
\draw[thick] (0,0) -- (1,1);

% S' region
\draw[thick] (0,0) -- (2,0.5);

% L segment (dotted box)
\draw[dotted] (0.7,0.7) -- (0.7,1) -- (1,1) -- (1,0.7) -- cycle;

% L curve (wiggly)
\draw[thick] (0,1) .. controls (0.2,0.95) and (0.5,0.8) .. (0.7,0.7);

% Labels
\node at (1.5,0.3) {$S'$};
\node at (0.5,0.5) {$S$};
\node at (0.8,1.05) {$L$};
\end{tikzpicture}
```

Let $S :=\left\{ \left( v_{1},v_{2} \right) \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2} \text{ : } v_{1}+v_{2}\leq 1 \right\}$ and $S :=\left\{ \left( v_{1},v_{2} \right) \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2} \text{ : } v_{1}+2v_{2}\leq 2 \right\}$.

By the way of contradiction, suppose there is a pair $\left( u_{1},u_{2},M,M' \right)$, such that

$$
\begin{align}
 & B_{M}:=\left\{ \left( u_{1}\left( x_{1} \right),u_{2}\left( x_{2} \right) \right) \text{ : }x_{1}+x_{2}=M \right\}=S & B_{M'}:=\left\{ \left( u_{1}\left( x_{1} \right),u_{2}\left( x_{2} \right) \right) \text{ : }x_{1}+x_{2}=M' \right\}=S'
\end{align}
$$

From $B_{M}=S$, we have

$$
\begin{align}
 & \frac{du_{2}}{du_{1}} = -\frac{\frac{du_{2}}{dx_{2}}}{\frac{du_{1}}{dx_{1}}}=-1 \\
 \implies & u_{1}'\left( x_{1} \right) = u_{2}'\left( x_{2} \right) = a \geq0 \text{ , } \forall x_{1},x_{2} \leq M
\end{align}
$$

Notice that all points $\left( u_{1}^{L},u_{2}^{L} \right)$ on the segment $L$ represents the amount of good assigned to each agent is less than $M$, according to monotone of utilities:

$$
\begin{align}
u_{1}^{L}=u_{1}\left( x_{1}^{L} \right) \leq u_{1}\left( M \right)  & \implies & x_{1}^{L}\leq M \\
u_{2}^{L}=u_{2}\left( x_{2}^{L} \right) \leq u_{2}\left( M \right)  & \implies & x_{2}^{L}\leq M \\
\end{align}
$$

Thus, along the segment $L$, we have

$$
\begin{align} \\
 & u_{1}'\left( x_{1}^{L} \right)=u_{2}'\left( x_{2}^{L} \right)=a \\
\implies & \frac{du_{2}^{L}}{du_{1}^{L}}=-\frac{u_{2}'\left( x_{2}^{L} \right)}{u_{1}'\left( x_{1}^{L}\right)}=-1
\end{align}
$$

which is a contradiction to the slope of $S'$: $\frac{du_{2}^{L}}{du_{1}^{L}}=-\frac{1}{2}$. QED

The intuition here is the 2 subset should look "parallel" in the sense of same substitution ratio for the segment of $S'$ which is dominated by the aspiration of $S$ as well as that for segment of $S$, i.e. the same slope on $L:=\left\{ u\in  \partial S' \text{ : } u_{i}\leq a_{i}\left( S \right), \forall i \in I:=\left\{ 1,2 \right\} \right\}$ and $\partial S$.

Although there might not always be a pair $\left\{ u_{1},u_{2},M,M' \right\}$ with $M,M'\in \mathbb{R}_{+}$ to represent any 2 arbitrary convex and compact sets $S,S' \subset \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$, there could be a pair $\left\{ u_{1},u_{2},M,M' \right\}$ with $M,M'\in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}$ to represent $S,S'$, for $L\geq 2$ (the number of good is no smaller than 2).
