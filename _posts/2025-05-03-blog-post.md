## The uniform rule is the only efficient rule whose range of allocations is the smallest.

Denote the uniform rule as $\displaystyle U$ and an alternative Pareto efficient rule as $\displaystyle \phi \in P, \phi\neq U$. The following is to show $\displaystyle \mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in N}\phi_{i}-\mathop{\min}\limits_{i \in N}\phi_{i}>\mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in N}U_{i}-\mathop{\min}\limits_{i \in N}U_{i}$

Without loss of generality, suppose we are in the insufficient endowment case: $\displaystyle \sum_{i \in N}P_{i}>M \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{1}$. Then the uniform rule is $\displaystyle U_{i}=\mathop{\min}\left\{ P_{i},\lambda \right\}$ such that $\displaystyle \sum_{i}U_{i}=M$, and $\displaystyle \mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in N}U_{i}=\lambda$. Additionally, for all $\displaystyle i$, $\displaystyle \phi_{i}\leq P_{i}$

By the way of contradiction, suppose $\displaystyle \mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in N}\phi_{i}-\mathop{\min}\limits_{i \in N}\phi_{i}\leq\mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in N}U_{i}-\mathop{\min}\limits_{i \in N}U_{i}$. 

1. If $\displaystyle \mathop{\max}\limits_{i}\phi_{i}\leq\mathop{\max}\limits_{i}U_{i}=\lambda$, then
	$$
	\begin{align}
	 & \phi_{i}\leq P_{i}    \cap  \phi_{i}\leq \mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in N} \phi_{i}\leq \mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in N}U_{i}=\lambda   \\
	\implies  & \phi_{i}\leq \mathop{\min}\left\{ P_{i}, \lambda \right\}=U_{i} \\
	\phi\neq U\implies &  \sum_{i}\phi_{i}< \sum_{i}U_{i}=M
	\end{align}
	$$
	which is in contradiction with $\displaystyle \phi$ is a fully allocation.
2. If $\displaystyle \mathop{\max}\limits_{i}\phi_{i}>\mathop{\max}\limits_{i}U_{i}=\lambda$, to ensure $\displaystyle \phi$ has smaller range, it must be $\displaystyle \mathop{\min}\limits_{_{i}}\phi_{i}> \mathop{\min}\limits_{_{i}}U_{i}=\mathop{\min}\limits_{_{i}}P_{i}$. 
	However, it is a Pareto improvement to decrease the allocation for the agent $\displaystyle i^{\star}$ who is equipped with the lowest peak $\displaystyle P_{i^{\star}}=\mathop{\min}\limits_{i}P_{i}$ and increase the allocation of another agent $\displaystyle j$ with $\displaystyle \phi_{j}<P_{j}$. Thus, $\displaystyle \phi$ is not Pareto efficient. Contradiction!


## The uniform rule is the only efficient rule whose variance of allocations is the smallest.

Denote the uniform rule as $\displaystyle U$ and an alternative Pareto efficient rule as $\displaystyle \phi \in P, \phi\neq U$. The following is to show $\displaystyle var\left( \phi_{i} \right)>var\left( U_{i} \right)$

Without loss of generality, suppose we are in the insufficient endowment case: $\displaystyle \sum_{i \in N}P_{i}>M \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{1}$. Then the uniform rule is $\displaystyle U_{i}=\mathop{\min}\left\{ P_{i},\lambda \right\}$ such that $\displaystyle \sum_{i}U_{i}=M$, and $\displaystyle \mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in N}U_{i}=\lambda$. It is obvious that $\displaystyle \lambda\geq \frac{M}{N}$.

Additionally, for all $\displaystyle i$, $\displaystyle \phi_{i}\leq P_{i}$. Then we have
$$
\sum_{i:P_{i}<\lambda}\phi_{i}\leq\sum_{i:P_{i}<\lambda}P_{i}=\sum_{i:P_{i}<\lambda}U_{i}
$$
From feasible condition $\displaystyle \sum_{i \in N}\phi_{i}=\sum_{i \in N}U_{i}=M$, we have
$$
\frac{1}{\sum_{i \in N}\mathbb{I}\{ P_{i}\geq \lambda \}}\sum_{i:P_{i}\geq \lambda}\phi_{i} \geq \frac{1}{\sum_{i \in N}\mathbb{I}\{ P_{i}\geq \lambda \}}\sum_{i:P_{i}\geq \lambda}U_{i}=\lambda\geq \frac{M}{N}
$$

### Case 1: given $\displaystyle \phi_{i}=P_{i}$ for all $\displaystyle P_{i} \in \left( \frac{M}{N}, \lambda \right)$ 
$$
\begin{align}
var\left( \phi_{i} \right) & = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i \in N}\left( \phi_{i} -\frac{M}{N}\right)^{2} \\
 & = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i:P_{i}<\lambda}\left( \phi_{i}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} + \frac{\sum_{i \in N}\mathbb{I}\{ P_{i}\geq \lambda \}}{N}\sum_{i:P_{i}\geq\lambda}\left( \phi_{i}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}  \\
 & \geq  \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i:P_{i}<\lambda}\left( \phi_{i}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} + \frac{\sum_{i \in N}\mathbb{I}\{ P_{i}\geq \lambda \}}{N}\left( \frac{1}{\sum_{i \in N}\mathbb{I}\{ P_{i}\geq \lambda \}} \sum_{i:P_{i}\geq\lambda}\phi_{i}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}  \\
 & \geq \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i:P_{i}<\lambda}\left( U_{i}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} + \frac{\sum_{i \in N}\mathbb{I}\{ P_{i}\geq \lambda \}}{N}\left( \lambda-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}  \\
 & = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i:P_{i}<\lambda}\left( U_{i}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} + \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i: P_{i}\geq \lambda}\left( U_{i}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}  \\ \\
 & = var\left( U_{i} \right)
\end{align}
$$
where the 1st inequality comes from Jensen's inequality, and the 2nd comes from our previous result.

### Case 2: If there is some $\displaystyle i$ such that $\displaystyle \phi_{i}<P_{i}$ and $\displaystyle P_{i} \in \left[ \frac{M}{N},\lambda \right]$
Consider the following example, which is trying to construct a Pareto efficient allocation $\displaystyle \phi$ equipped with the minimum variance.

|                                           | Agent 1               | (Mean)                                     | Agent 2 - Agent N-1         | Agent N                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Efficient allocation $\displaystyle \phi$ | $\displaystyle P_{1}$ | $\displaystyle \left( \frac{M}{N} \right)$ | $\displaystyle P_{2}$       | $\displaystyle \lambda$  |
| Uniform allocation                        | $\displaystyle P_{1}$ | $\displaystyle \left( \frac{M}{N} \right)$ | $\displaystyle \frac{M}{N}$ | $\displaystyle \phi_{3}$ |
where $\displaystyle P_{1}<\frac{M}{N}<P_{2}<\lambda<\phi_{3}$.
From fully allocation condition, we have
$$
\begin{align}
P_{1}+\left( N-2 \right)P_{2}+\lambda=M  & \implies & \left( P_{1}-\frac{M}{N} \right)+\left( \lambda-\frac{M}{N} \right)+\left( N-2 \right)\left( P_{2}-\frac{M}{N} \right)=0\\
P_{1}+\left( N-2 \right)\frac{M}{N}+\phi_{3}=M & \implies & \left( P_{1}-\frac{M}{N} \right)+\left( \phi_{3}-\frac{M}{N} \right)=0\\
\end{align}
$$
From $\displaystyle \lambda>P_{2}$, and the fully allocation condition for Uniform rule, we have
$$
\frac{M}{N}-P_{1}>\left( N-1 \right)\left( P_{2}-\frac{M}{N} \right)
$$
The variances of $\displaystyle \phi$ and $\displaystyle U$ are
$$
\begin{align}
Nvar\left( \phi \right) & =\left( P_{1}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}+\left( \phi_{3}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}=2\left( P_{1}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} \\
Nvar\left( U \right) & =\left( P_{1}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}+\left( N-2 \right)\left( P_{2}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}+\left( \lambda-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} \\
 & =2\left( P_{1}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}+\left( N-2 \right)\left( N-1 \right)\left( P_{2}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} \\
 & +2\left( N-2 \right)\left(P_{1}-\frac{M}{N}  \right)\left( P_{2}-\frac{M}{N} \right) \\
 & <2\left( P_{1}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}+\left( N-2 \right)\left( N-1 \right)\left( P_{2}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}  \\
 & -2\left( N-2 \right)\left( N-1 \right)\left( P_{2}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} \\
 & =2\left( P_{1}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2}-\left( N-2 \right)\left( N-1 \right)\left( P_{2}-\frac{M}{N} \right)^{2} \\
 & <Nvar\left( \phi \right)
\end{align}
$$
As the efficient allocation $\displaystyle \phi$ is trying to induce the deviation of allocations on agent $\displaystyle i$ whose peak $\displaystyle P_{i}\in\left[ \frac{M}{N},\lambda \right]$, the decreased amount would be compensated by agents who are further from $\displaystyle \frac{M}{N}$. By convexity of square function and Jensen's inequality, this modification on $\displaystyle \phi$ would induce a increase in variance. Thus, for all efficient allocation $\displaystyle \phi$, it should be $\displaystyle \phi_{i}=P_{i}$ for all $\displaystyle P_{i}\in\left[ \frac{M}{N}, \lambda \right]$ to minimize its variance. Then we could go back to case 1.
