## The uniform rule is the only efficient rule whose range of allocations is the smallest.

Denote the uniform rule as <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle U$</span> and an alternative Pareto efficient rule as <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi &#92;in P, &#92;phi&#92;neq U$</span>. The following is to show <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;phi&#95;{i}-&#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;phi&#95;{i}>&#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}U&#95;{i}-&#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}U&#95;{i}$</span>

Without loss of generality, suppose we are in the insufficient endowment case: <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}P&#95;{i}>M &#92;in &#92;mathbb{R}&#95;{+}^{1}$</span>. Then the uniform rule is <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle U&#95;{i}=&#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;left&#92;{ P&#95;{i},&#92;lambda &#92;right&#92;}$</span> such that <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;sum&#95;{i}U&#95;{i}=M$</span>, and <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}U&#95;{i}=&#92;lambda$</span>. Additionally, for all <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle i$</span>, <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq P&#95;{i}$</span>

By the way of contradiction, suppose <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;phi&#95;{i}-&#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq&#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}U&#95;{i}-&#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}U&#95;{i}$</span>. 

1. If <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i}&#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq&#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i}U&#95;{i}=&#92;lambda$</span>, then
	<div class="math">$$
	&#92;begin{align}
	 & &#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq P&#95;{i}    &#92;cap  &#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq &#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N} &#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq &#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}U&#95;{i}=&#92;lambda   &#92;&#92;
	&#92;implies  & &#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq &#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;left&#92;{ P&#95;{i}, &#92;lambda &#92;right&#92;}=U&#95;{i} &#92;&#92;
	&#92;phi&#92;neq U&#92;implies &  &#92;sum&#95;{i}&#92;phi&#95;{i}< &#92;sum&#95;{i}U&#95;{i}=M
	&#92;end{align}
	$$</div>
	which is in contradiction with <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi$</span> is a fully allocation.
2. If <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i}&#92;phi&#95;{i}>&#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i}U&#95;{i}=&#92;lambda$</span>, to ensure <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi$</span> has smaller range, it must be <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;limits&#95;{i}&#92;phi&#95;{i}> &#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;limits&#95;{i}U&#95;{i}=&#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;limits&#95;{i}P&#95;{i}$</span>. 
	However, it is a Pareto improvement to decrease the allocation for the agent <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle i^{&#92;star}$</span> who is equipped with the lowest peak <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle P&#95;{i^{&#92;star}}=&#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;limits&#95;{i}P&#95;{i}$</span> and increase the allocation of another agent <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle j$</span> with <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi&#95;{j}<P&#95;{j}$</span>. Thus, <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi$</span> is not Pareto efficient. Contradiction!


## The uniform rule is the only efficient rule whose variance of allocations is the smallest.

Denote the uniform rule as <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle U$</span> and an alternative Pareto efficient rule as <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi &#92;in P, &#92;phi&#92;neq U$</span>. The following is to show <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle var&#92;left( &#92;phi&#95;{i} &#92;right)>var&#92;left( U&#95;{i} &#92;right)$</span>

Without loss of generality, suppose we are in the insufficient endowment case: <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}P&#95;{i}>M &#92;in &#92;mathbb{R}&#95;{+}^{1}$</span>. Then the uniform rule is <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle U&#95;{i}=&#92;mathop{&#92;min}&#92;left&#92;{ P&#95;{i},&#92;lambda &#92;right&#92;}$</span> such that <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;sum&#95;{i}U&#95;{i}=M$</span>, and <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;mathop{&#92;max}&#92;limits&#95;{i &#92;in N}U&#95;{i}=&#92;lambda$</span>. It is obvious that <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;lambda&#92;geq &#92;frac{M}{N}$</span>.

Additionally, for all <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle i$</span>, <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq P&#95;{i}$</span>. Then we have
<div class="math">$$
&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}<&#92;lambda}&#92;phi&#95;{i}&#92;leq&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}<&#92;lambda}P&#95;{i}=&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}<&#92;lambda}U&#95;{i}
$$</div>
From feasible condition <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;phi&#95;{i}=&#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}U&#95;{i}=M$</span>, we have
<div class="math">$$
&#92;frac{1}{&#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;mathbb{I}&#92;{ P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda &#92;}}&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda}&#92;phi&#95;{i} &#92;geq &#92;frac{1}{&#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;mathbb{I}&#92;{ P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda &#92;}}&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda}U&#95;{i}=&#92;lambda&#92;geq &#92;frac{M}{N}
$$</div>

### Case 1: given <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi&#95;{i}=P&#95;{i}$</span> for all <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle P&#95;{i} &#92;in &#92;left( &#92;frac{M}{N}, &#92;lambda &#92;right)$</span> 
<div class="math">$$
&#92;begin{align}
var&#92;left( &#92;phi&#95;{i} &#92;right) & = &#92;frac{1}{N}&#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;left( &#92;phi&#95;{i} -&#92;frac{M}{N}&#92;right)^{2} &#92;&#92;
 & = &#92;frac{1}{N}&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}<&#92;lambda}&#92;left( &#92;phi&#95;{i}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} + &#92;frac{&#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;mathbb{I}&#92;{ P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda &#92;}}{N}&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}&#92;geq&#92;lambda}&#92;left( &#92;phi&#95;{i}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}  &#92;&#92;
 & &#92;geq  &#92;frac{1}{N}&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}<&#92;lambda}&#92;left( &#92;phi&#95;{i}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} + &#92;frac{&#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;mathbb{I}&#92;{ P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda &#92;}}{N}&#92;left( &#92;frac{1}{&#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;mathbb{I}&#92;{ P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda &#92;}} &#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}&#92;geq&#92;lambda}&#92;phi&#95;{i}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}  &#92;&#92;
 & &#92;geq &#92;frac{1}{N}&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}<&#92;lambda}&#92;left( U&#95;{i}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} + &#92;frac{&#92;sum&#95;{i &#92;in N}&#92;mathbb{I}&#92;{ P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda &#92;}}{N}&#92;left( &#92;lambda-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}  &#92;&#92;
 & = &#92;frac{1}{N}&#92;sum&#95;{i:P&#95;{i}<&#92;lambda}&#92;left( U&#95;{i}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} + &#92;frac{1}{N}&#92;sum&#95;{i: P&#95;{i}&#92;geq &#92;lambda}&#92;left( U&#95;{i}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}  &#92;&#92; &#92;&#92;
 & = var&#92;left( U&#95;{i} &#92;right)
&#92;end{align}
$$</div>
where the 1st inequality comes from Jensen's inequality, and the 2nd comes from our previous result.

### Case 2: If there is some <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle i$</span> such that <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi&#95;{i}<P&#95;{i}$</span> and <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle P&#95;{i} &#92;in &#92;left[ &#92;frac{M}{N},&#92;lambda &#92;right]$</span>
Consider the following example, which is trying to construct a Pareto efficient allocation <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi$</span> equipped with the minimum variance.

where <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle P&#95;{1}<&#92;frac{M}{N}<P&#95;{2}<&#92;lambda<&#92;phi&#95;{3}$</span>.
From fully allocation condition, we have
<div class="math">$$
&#92;begin{align}
P&#95;{1}+&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)P&#95;{2}+&#92;lambda=M  & &#92;implies & &#92;left( P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)+&#92;left( &#92;lambda-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)+&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)&#92;left( P&#95;{2}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)=0&#92;&#92;
P&#95;{1}+&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)&#92;frac{M}{N}+&#92;phi&#95;{3}=M & &#92;implies & &#92;left( P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)+&#92;left( &#92;phi&#95;{3}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)=0&#92;&#92;
&#92;end{align}
$$</div>
From <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;lambda>P&#95;{2}$</span>, and the fully allocation condition for Uniform rule, we have
<div class="math">$$
&#92;frac{M}{N}-P&#95;{1}>&#92;left( N-1 &#92;right)&#92;left( P&#95;{2}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)
$$</div>
The variances of <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi$</span> and <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle U$</span> are
<div class="math">$$
&#92;begin{align}
Nvar&#92;left( &#92;phi &#92;right) & =&#92;left( P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}+&#92;left( &#92;phi&#95;{3}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}=2&#92;left( P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} &#92;&#92;
Nvar&#92;left( U &#92;right) & =&#92;left( P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}+&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)&#92;left( P&#95;{2}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}+&#92;left( &#92;lambda-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} &#92;&#92;
 & =2&#92;left( P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}+&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)&#92;left( N-1 &#92;right)&#92;left( P&#95;{2}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} &#92;&#92;
 & +2&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)&#92;left(P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N}  &#92;right)&#92;left( P&#95;{2}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right) &#92;&#92;
 & <2&#92;left( P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}+&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)&#92;left( N-1 &#92;right)&#92;left( P&#95;{2}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}  &#92;&#92;
 & -2&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)&#92;left( N-1 &#92;right)&#92;left( P&#95;{2}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} &#92;&#92;
 & =2&#92;left( P&#95;{1}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2}-&#92;left( N-2 &#92;right)&#92;left( N-1 &#92;right)&#92;left( P&#95;{2}-&#92;frac{M}{N} &#92;right)^{2} &#92;&#92;
 & <Nvar&#92;left( &#92;phi &#92;right)
&#92;end{align}
$$</div>
As the efficient allocation <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi$</span> is trying to induce the deviation of allocations on agent <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle i$</span> whose peak <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle P&#95;{i}&#92;in&#92;left[ &#92;frac{M}{N},&#92;lambda &#92;right]$</span>, the decreased amount would be compensated by agents who are further from <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;frac{M}{N}$</span>. By convexity of square function and Jensen's inequality, this modification on <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi$</span> would induce a increase in variance. Thus, for all efficient allocation <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi$</span>, it should be <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;phi&#95;{i}=P&#95;{i}$</span> for all <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle P&#95;{i}&#92;in&#92;left[ &#92;frac{M}{N}, &#92;lambda &#92;right]$</span> to minimize its variance. Then we could go back to case 1.
