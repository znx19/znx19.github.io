# The $\displaystyle k$-th maximum of a group continuous functions $\displaystyle f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n}$ is continuous for all $\displaystyle i \in I:=\left\{ 1,2,\dots,n \right\}$.

Step 1: The maximum function $\displaystyle \mathop{\max}\left\{ f,g \right\}=g+ \frac{f-g+|f-g|}{2}$ is continuous since absolute value operator preserves continuity of function. By induction, the maximum function $\displaystyle \mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in I}\left\{ f_{i} \right\}$ is continuous for finitely many continuous functions $\displaystyle (f_{i})_{i \in I}$

Step 2: The minimum function $\displaystyle \mathop{\min}\limits_{i \in I}\left\{ f_{i} \right\}=-\mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in I}\left\{ -f_{i} \right\}$ is continuous since maximum operator preserves continuity.

Step 3: For all $\displaystyle k \in I$, the $\displaystyle k$-th maximum function $\displaystyle \mathop{\max\left( k \right)}\limits_{i \in I}\left\{ f_{i} \right\}=\mathop{\min}\limits_{I' \subset I, |I'|=n-k+1}\left\{ \mathop{\max}\limits_{i \in I'}\left\{ f_{i} \right\} \right\}$ is continuous since both minimum and maximum operators preserve continuity of functions.
