## Set up:
- 2 agent & 2 good Edgeworth economy
- Total resource: $\displaystyle M=\left( M_{1},M_{2} \right)\in \mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}$
## Part 1: If preferences are continuous, strictly monotonic, strictly convex, and homothetic, then the Pareto set is either a doubly visible curve or a segment connecting origins.
### Case 1: If there is a point $\displaystyle x=\left( x_{1},x_{2} \right)$ from Pareto set on the segment connecting the origins: $\exists x=\left( x_{1},x_{2} \right)\in P, s.t. \displaystyle \frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}=\frac{M_{2}}{M_{1}}$, then the Pareto set is the segment connecting origins: $\displaystyle \forall y \in P, \frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}}=\frac{M_{2}}{M_{1}}$

By the way of contradiction, suppose there is a point $\displaystyle x$ from Pareto set on the segment from $\displaystyle O_{1}$ to $\displaystyle O_{2}$, and there is another point $\displaystyle y$ from Pareto set not on that segment.
Since both 2 preferences are strictly convex, the Pareto equivalent class must be singleton. Thus, from the perspective of agent 1, either $\displaystyle y$ is preferred to $\displaystyle x$, or the other way, holds. Without loss of generality, suppose $\displaystyle y$ is preferred to x for agent 1, $\displaystyle y>_{1}x$.
Since both $\displaystyle x$ and $\displaystyle y$ are Pareto efficient, we have x is preferred to y for agent 2, $\displaystyle y<_{2}x$.
Denote another 2 allocations on the segment connecting origins as $\displaystyle z$ and $\displaystyle t$, such that z is equivalent to $\displaystyle y$ for agent 2, $\displaystyle z \sim_{2}y$ and $\displaystyle t$ is equivalent to $\displaystyle y$ for agent 1, $\displaystyle t\sim_{1}y$.
From homothetic preference of agent 2, we have $\displaystyle z\geq t$. Otherwise, we could find an allocation $\displaystyle s$ satisfying $\displaystyle s\sim_{2}t$ from $\displaystyle z\sim_{2}y$, denoted by red points in the following graph. 
![[Pasted image 20250405161631.png]]
Notice that $\displaystyle s$ is on the segment from $\displaystyle y$ to $\displaystyle O_{2}$, then $\displaystyle s>y$. From strict monotone of agent 1's preference, he should prefer $\displaystyle s$ to $\displaystyle y$: $\displaystyle s>_{1}y$. Given $\displaystyle y\sim_{2}t$, we have $\displaystyle s$ Pareto dominates y, leading to a contradiction to the efficiency of $\displaystyle y$.

If $\displaystyle z>t$, then $\displaystyle z$ Pareto dominates$\displaystyle y$, contradicting to the efficiency of $\displaystyle y$. Otherwise, $\displaystyle z=t$, a strictly convex combination of $\displaystyle z$ and $\displaystyle y$ also dominates $\displaystyle y$ from strict convex of 2 preferences, again contradicting to the efficiency of $\displaystyle y$. 

### Case 2: If there is not a point $\displaystyle x=\left( x_{1},x_{2} \right)$ on the segment connecting the origins: $\displaystyle \forall x \in P, \frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\not=\frac{M_{2}}{M_{1}}$, then the Pareto set is doubly visible: $\displaystyle \forall x,y \in P, \frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\not=\frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}}\cap\frac{M_2-x_{2}}{M_{1}-x_{1}}\not=\frac{M_{2}-y_{2}}{M_{1}-y_{1}}$

By the way of contradiction, suppose there is are 2 points $\displaystyle x$ and $\displaystyle y$ from Pareto set on the same line starting from $\displaystyle O_{1}$. Without loss of generality, suppose $\displaystyle y>x$.

Denote another 2 allocations on the segment connecting $\displaystyle x$ and $\displaystyle O_{2}$ as $\displaystyle z$ and $\displaystyle t$, such that z is equivalent to $\displaystyle y$ for agent 2, $\displaystyle z \sim_{2}y$ and $\displaystyle t$ is equivalent to $\displaystyle y$ for agent 1, $\displaystyle t\sim_{1}y$.
From homothetic preference of agent 2, we have $\displaystyle z\geq t$. Otherwise, we could find an allocation $\displaystyle s$ satisfying $\displaystyle s\sim_{2}t$ from $\displaystyle z\sim_{2}y$, denoted by red points in the following graph. 
![[Pasted image 20250405161653.png]]
Notice that $\displaystyle s$ is on the segment from $\displaystyle y$ to $\displaystyle O_{2}$, then $\displaystyle s>y$. From strict monotone of agent 1's preference, he should prefer $\displaystyle s$ to $\displaystyle y$: $\displaystyle s>_{1}y$. Given $\displaystyle y\sim_{2}t$, we have $\displaystyle s$ Pareto dominates y, leading to a contradiction to the efficiency of $\displaystyle y$.

If $\displaystyle z>t$, then $\displaystyle z$ Pareto dominates$\displaystyle y$, contradicting to the efficiency of $\displaystyle y$. Otherwise, $\displaystyle z=t$, a strictly convex combination of $\displaystyle z$ and $\displaystyle y$ also dominates $\displaystyle y$ from strict convex of 2 preferences, again contradicting to the efficiency of $\displaystyle y$. 

The proof on the visibility from the other origin $\displaystyle O_{2}$ is similar, and there is no need to repeat this process again.
