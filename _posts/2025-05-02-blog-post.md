## The Walrasian rule is not immune to cooperation, even for homothetic or quasi-linear preferences. 

The following is an example on utility gains from cooperation on endowments for homothetic (resp. quasi-linear) preferences.

where <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle &#92;epsilon>0$</span> is positive and close to 0.

Given 2 slopes <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle P,P'$</span> of indifference curve on 2 different points <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle W&#95;{3},W&#95;{3}'$</span>, it is easy to construct <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle u&#95;{3}$</span> such that it is homothetic or quasi-linear. And linear preferences <span class="math">$&#92;displaystyle u&#95;{1},u&#95;{2}$</span> are both homothetic and quasi-linear.
![[Pasted image 20250430175027.png]]
Finally, agent 1 and 2 could enjoy a strict welfare gain by reshuffling their endowments even in homothetic (resp. quasi-linear) case.

However, with restriction on preferences which are both homothetic and quasi-linear (which is equivalent to linear preferences), 2 agents can not strictly enhance their utilities by shuffling their endowment. A technical proof is omitted here, since it is not the main part of this question.
