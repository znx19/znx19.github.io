## The Walrasian rule is not immune to cooperation, even for homothetic or quasi-linear preferences. 

The following is an example on utility gains from cooperation on endowments for homothetic (resp. quasi-linear) preferences.

![[Pasted image 20250428101304.png]]

| Preference                                                                | Endowment                                | $\displaystyle P=\left( 1,1 \right)$     | Reshuffled endowment                      | $\displaystyle P'=\left( \frac{1}{2+\epsilon},1 \right)$                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| $\displaystyle u_{1}=x_{1}+6x_{2}$                                        | $\displaystyle e_{1}=\left( 3,1 \right)$ | $\displaystyle W_{1}=\left( 0,4 \right)$ | $\displaystyle e'_{1}=\left( 1,4 \right)$ | $\displaystyle W_{1}'=\left( 0,4+\frac{1}{2+\epsilon} \right)$          |
| $\displaystyle u_{2}=6x_{1}+x_{2}$                                        | $\displaystyle e_{2}=\left( 0,4 \right)$ | $\displaystyle W_{2}=\left( 4,0 \right)$ | $\displaystyle e'_{2}=\left( 2,1 \right)$ | $\displaystyle W_{2}'=\left( 4+\epsilon,0 \right)$                      |
| $\displaystyle u_{3}$ can be constructed to be homothetic or quasi-linear | $\displaystyle e_{3}=\left( 3,1 \right)$ | $\displaystyle W_{3}=\left( 2,2 \right)$ | $\displaystyle e_{3}=\left( 3,1 \right)$  | $\displaystyle W_{3}'=\left( 2-\epsilon,2-\frac{1}{2+\epsilon} \right)$ |
where $\displaystyle \epsilon>0$ is positive and close to 0.

Given 2 slopes $\displaystyle P,P'$ of indifference curve on 2 different points $\displaystyle W_{3},W_{3}'$, it is easy to construct $\displaystyle u_{3}$ such that it is homothetic or quasi-linear. And linear preferences $\displaystyle u_{1},u_{2}$ are both homothetic and quasi-linear.
![[Pasted image 20250430175027.png]]
Finally, agent 1 and 2 could enjoy a strict welfare gain by reshuffling their endowments even in homothetic (resp. quasi-linear) case.

However, with restriction on preferences which are both homothetic and quasi-linear (which is equivalent to linear preferences), 2 agents can not strictly enhance their utilities by shuffling their endowment. A technical proof is omitted here, since it is not the main part of this question.
