## Walrasian rule is non-bossy in 2-good case if agent $\displaystyle i$ 's Walrasian allocation is not equal to his endowment $\displaystyle W_{i}\neq w_{i}$
Want to show: Given the number of good $\displaystyle L=2$, for all $\displaystyle R \in \mathcal{R}^{N}$, for all $\displaystyle i \in N$ and for all $\displaystyle R_{i} \in \mathcal{R}$, if $\displaystyle W_{i}\left( R_{i}, R_{-i} \right)=W_{i}\left( R_{i}',R_{-i} \right)\neq w_{i}$, then $\displaystyle W\left( R_{i}, R_{-i} \right)=W\left( R_{i}',R_{-i} \right)$

Denote the equilibrium price vector for 2 Walrasian allocation $\displaystyle W,W'$ as $\displaystyle P,P' \in \Delta^{1}$, which are in the simplex with degree of freedom equal to 1. Consider oth 2 preferences of agent $\displaystyle i$ are strictly monotonic $\displaystyle R_{i}, R_{i}' \in \mathcal{R_{sm}}$. By Walrasian rule, both 2 allocations $\displaystyle W_{i} W_{i}'$ are on the budget lines in each case: 
$$
\begin{align}
p \cdot W_{i}=p \cdot e_{i} &  & p' \cdot W_{i}=p' \cdot e_{i}
\end{align}
$$
From $\displaystyle W_{i}=W_{i}'$, we have
$$
\left( p-p' \right) \cdot \left( W_{i}-e_{i} \right)=0
$$
Since $\displaystyle W_{i}-e_{i}\in R^{2}\setminus \{ 0 \}$ and $\displaystyle p-p'\in \Delta^{1}$, it is obvious that $\displaystyle p=p'$ from the axiom in Euclidean geometry that there is a unique line connecting 2 distinct points $\displaystyle W_{i}, e_{i}$ in 2 dimensional space.

For another agent $\displaystyle j \neq i$, his preference $\displaystyle R_{j}$, endowment $\displaystyle W_{j}$ and the price index $\displaystyle p'=p$ all remain the same, then $\displaystyle W_{j}$ still solves the new utility maximization problem: $\displaystyle W_{j}=W_{j}'$. From the market clearing condition $\displaystyle \sum_{i \in N}W_{i}=\sum_{i \in N}W_{i}'=\sum_{i \in N}e_{i}$, we could verify that $\displaystyle W_{j}'=W_{j}$ is the Walrasian allocation under $\displaystyle (R_{i}',R_{-i})$. 

Thus, $\displaystyle W=W'$.

## Walrasian rule is not non-bossy in 2-good case if agent $\displaystyle i$ 's Walrasian allocation is equal to his endowment $\displaystyle W_{i} = e_{i}$
From above proof, if $\displaystyle W_{i}=e_{i}$, the equation $\displaystyle (p-p')\cdot\left( W_{i}-e_{i} \right)=0$ holds for all $\displaystyle p,p'\in \Delta^{1}$. Then it is not necessarily to have $\displaystyle p=p'$. The following is an example not satisfying non-bossy condition.

## Walrasian rule is not non-bossy for more goods 
From above proof, there are multiple $\displaystyle p,p' \in \Delta^{L-1>1}$ to ensure $\displaystyle (p-p')\cdot\left( W_{i}-e_{i} \right)=0$ with $\displaystyle W_{i}-e_{i} \in \mathbb{R}^{L}$, based on the axiom that there are multiple hyperplanes connecting 2 points $\displaystyle W_{i}, e_{i}$ in $\displaystyle L\left( L>2 \right)$ dimensional space. Then it is not necessarily to have $\displaystyle p=p'$. The following is an example not satisfying non-bossy condition.
